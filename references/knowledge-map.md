# Knowledge map and recommendations

## Build meaningful nodes

Represent nodes as problems, concepts, methods, events, thinkers, or works. Organize them into flexible domains only for navigation; never treat a category checklist as knowledge.

Weight a node from demonstrated evidence:

- exposure: encountered but not retrieved;
- working: explained once with support;
- connected: compared accurately with another idea;
- usable: applied or critiqued after a delay.

Record edges with a direction and relation such as `supports`, `contradicts`, `applies`, `historically-contextualizes`, `shares-method`, or `refines`. Mark the evidence source and confidence. Do not assert a relationship merely because two books share a category.

## Generate a review

Summarize:

1. strongest usable clusters with evidence;
2. fragile or isolated nodes;
3. missing prerequisite knowledge that blocks current goals;
4. one promising cross-domain bridge;
5. one next learning move.

Prefer a compact text map over false precision. Explain uncertainty and invite corrections to reading history.

## Recommend the next reading

Infer the job the next resource must do: add context, deepen theory, provide an opposing view, supply evidence, teach a method, or broaden transfer. Offer no more than three resources. For each state:

- the exact gap it addresses;
- its link to a concept the user has demonstrated;
- difficulty and expected commitment;
- why it should be read now;
- a guiding question.

Favor sequencing over prestige. If the prerequisite gap is narrow, recommend a chapter, essay, lecture, or reference entry instead of a whole book. When bibliographic details or availability may be uncertain, verify them before recommending.
